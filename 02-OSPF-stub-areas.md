# OSPF Stub Areas

**What is a stub area?**
- A regular OSPF area contains all the LSAs that are generated by the routers in that area, and also the LSAs that are generated by the ABRs and ASBRs that are connected to that area.
A stub area is an OSPF area that does not contain an ASBR and thus do not mediate external connectivity to an entire OSPF domain, and instead relies on a default route to reach external destinations. 

If the area contains a ABR that is configured as a stub, the following happens:
- The ABR will not advertise any Type 4 and Type 5 LSAs in this area
- Every internal router in the stubby area will ignore any received type 5 LSAs, they wont originate any type 5 LSAs.
- ABR will automatically inject a default route into the area as a type 3 LSA.

**What are the usecases for stub areas?**
- To reduce the overhead of OSPF areas that do not need to know about the entire topology of the network, for example a branch office that only needs to know how to reach the rest of the network, but does not need to know about all the details of the network.
